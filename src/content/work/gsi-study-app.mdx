<Section title="Problem" fig="1" figLabel="review section before the midterm">
I had one section to help students get ready for a midterm built on term definitions, primary sources, and an argued essay. Paper review sheets do not tell me who used them or what students still get wrong. I wanted a tool students would actually open, and that would show me where the class was weakest while there was still time to fix it.
</Section>

<Section title="How it works" fig="5" figLabel="study decks">
- **Study on your own.** Five decks: key terms, primary sources, historical agents, a quick-recall round, and an essay builder. Each card is marked "got it" or "review again", and missed cards come back until they are mastered.
- **Essay builder.** Walks a student through an essay one step at a time: thesis, three body paragraphs, the primary source, the conclusion, and a final checklist.
- **Play together.** A host view on the projector and a live room on students' phones: teams, buzzers, and scores, synced in real time.
- **Instructor dashboard.** Deck usage, the most-missed cards, and where students drop off in the essay builder. It is password-protected and is the only code that reads usage data.
- **A printable guide** made from the same content, for students who prefer paper.
</Section>

<Section title="My role" fig="1" figLabel="source of content for app, game, and guide">
**I decided:** what to build and what to leave out; the content and the five decks; the review-again loop; that the game runs in section on a projector, not as homework; which signals the dashboard tracks, chosen to answer one question (what should I re-teach?); and the privacy rules below.

**AI built:** the app, the live room, the dashboard, and the guide, to my specs and under my review.
</Section>

<Section title="Decisions and trade-offs" fig="Berkeley" figLabel="sign-in only, enforced in the database">
**Locked to the class, at the database.** Sign-in is Google with a Berkeley account only, and the database itself rejects anything else, so a bug in the page cannot leak the content. The public page source holds no course material; it loads only after sign-in.

**Measure to teach, not to grade.** Usage is tied to study sessions and read only by the dashboard. It never feeds a grade, and nothing from it leaves the course. Public numbers, like the ones on this page, are aggregate only.

**One content file.** The decks, the game, and the printed guide all come from the same file, so a fix in one place fixes all three.
</Section>

<Section title="If I were the PM at a lab" fig="Re-teach" figLabel="the question the dashboard answers">
I'd instrument a learning tool around the decision the teacher has to make next, not around engagement. "Most-missed cards" and "where students drop off" change what happens in section tomorrow; time-in-app does not.
</Section>

<Section title="Limits">
- One course and one exam; students chose whether to use it, so users are self-selected.
- Usage shows practice, not learning. It cannot say whether the app changed anyone's grade, and it is not used to try.
</Section>
